%===== Abstract ===============


\begin{abstract}
    This project compares the variational quantum eigensolver (VQE)
    and digitized adiabatic quantum evolution for ground-state
    preparation in the transverse-field Ising model.
    Both algorithms are implemented and simulated classically
    using IBM's Qiskit, with and without simulated quantum noise.
    The results show that VQE achieves small ground-state energy
    errors, while adiabatic evolution achieves higher GHZ fidelity
    in the ideal case. With noise, however, the fidelity of
    adiabatic evolution generally decreases as the number of
    Trotter steps increases. The highest observed GHZ fidelity
    with noise is $F_{\mathrm{GHZ}}=0.951$, achieved with
    $T=10$ and $M=15$.
\end{abstract}



\maketitle

\section{Introduction}

The Ising model describes interacting spins on a lattice, commonly
with nearest-neighbor interactions. Introducing a transverse magnetic
field gives rise to the transverse-field Ising model, which provides
a simple system for studying quantum many-body physics.

Here, the model is used to compare two approaches to quantum
ground-state preparation: the variational quantum eigensolver
(VQE) and adiabatic quantum evolution.

\section{Model and methods}

The transverse-field Ising Hamiltonian is given by
\begin{align}
    H = -J \sum_{\langle i,j\rangle} Z_i Z_j
        -h \sum_i Z_i
        -g \sum_i X_i, \label{eq:ising_Hamiltonian}
\end{align}
where $X_i$ and $Z_i$ are Pauli operators acting on spin site $i$,
$J$ is the interaction strength, and $h$ and $g$ are the longitudinal and transverse field strengths, respectively.

The parameterized circuit used for state preparation starts from $n$ qubits initialized in $\ket{0}^{\otimes n}$.
A unitary $U(\vec{\theta})$, illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:state_preparation}, prepares the state
\begin{align}
    \ket{\psi(\vec{\theta})}
    =U(\vec{\theta})\ket{0}^{\otimes n}.
\end{align}

\begin{figure}[ht]
    \centering 
    \includegraphics[width=1.1\linewidth]{Figs/circuit.png}
    \caption{State preparation circuit for four qubits.}
    \label{fig:state_preparation}
\end{figure}

For example, $\vec{\theta}=\vec{0}$ gives $\ket{\psi(\vec{\theta})}=\ket{0}^{\otimes n}$ and $\vec{\theta} = (\pi/2, ..., \pi/2, 0, ..., 0)$, where there are $n$ angles equal $\pi/2$, and $3n$ angles equal $0$ ($\vec{\theta}$ has $4n$ parameters), gives $\ket{\psi(\vec{\theta})}=\ket{+}^{\otimes n}$. 

\subsection{Variational quantum eigensolver}

VQE uses the variational principle to estimate the ground-state energy $E_0$:
\begin{align}
    E(\boldsymbol{\theta})
    = \bra{\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})}
    H\ket{\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})}
    \geq E_0.
\end{align}
The circuit in Fig.~\ref{fig:state_preparation} prepares $\ket{\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})}$, while a classical optimizer updates \(\vec{\theta}\) to minimize the energy expectation value.


\subsection{Adiabatic quantum evolution}

Adiabatic evolution starts in the known ground state of an initial
Hamiltonian.
Here, we consider
\begin{align}
    H_i = -g_i\sum_i X_i,
\end{align}
whose ground state is $\ket{+}^{\otimes N}$ for $g_i>0$.

The Hamiltonian is gradually changed to the target Hamiltonian $H_f$ according to
\begin{align}
    H(s) = (1-s)H_i + sH_f,
    \qquad s = t/T,
\end{align}
where $T$ is the total evolution time. For sufficiently slow evolution and a large enough spectral gap, the final state approaches the ground state of $H_f$.
Here, $H$ is given by~\eqref{eq:ising_Hamiltonian}.

The evolution is discretized into $M$ time steps of duration $\Delta t=T/M$. At each step, the evolution operator is approximated using a first-order Lie--Trotter decomposition (see \href{https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/api/qiskit/qiskit.synthesis.LieTrotter}{Qiskit's LieTrotter}).
For a Hamiltonian $H=H_1+H_2$,
\begin{align}
    e^{-i\Delta t(H_1+H_2)}
    =e^{-i\Delta t H_1}e^{-i\Delta t H_2}
    +\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^2).
\end{align}
The approximation becomes more accurate for smaller time steps, at the cost of increasing the number of quantum gates.

\subsection{Code}

The code is available on GitHub~\cite{code}, with instructions for installation and execution provided in the README.

The transverse-field Ising Hamiltonian is implemented using Qiskit, supporting both one- and two-dimensional lattices. 
VQE and digitized adiabatic quantum evolution are simulated classically using Qiskit Aer. 
For comparison, the ground-state energy is also calculated by exact diagonalization.

\subsubsection{Noise}

Quantum noise is introduced using Qiskit Aer's noise simulation tools. 
The implementation supports several noise channels, including depolarizing errors, bit- and phase-flip errors, amplitude and phase damping, and thermal relaxation.
These can be applied with user-defined parameters to investigate their influence on the algorithms.

% In particular, thermal relaxation is characterized by
% the energy relaxation time $T_1$ and the coherence time $T_2$.
% The latter includes contributions from both energy relaxation
% and pure dephasing, characterized by $T_\phi$. Under a
% Markovian approximation, these times satisfy
% \begin{equation}
%     \frac{1}{T_2}
%     = \frac{1}{2T_1}+\frac{1}{T_\phi}.
% \end{equation}

Alternatively, a noise model can be constructed from IBM's 127-qubit \texttt{FakeSherbrooke} backend~\cite{fakesherbrooke}, which provides hardware-derived gate errors and relaxation parameters. This allows the algorithms to be investigated under more realistic, although still simulated, noise conditions.


The quantum circuits are transpiled to match the basis gates of the backend. 
However, the simulations are not intended to reproduce the performance of the physical device quantitatively, since its qubit connectivity and physical qubit layout are not fully accounted for. 
Further details on the implemented noise channels and their parameters are provided in the README~\cite{code}.



\section{Results}

\subsection{Comparison of VQE and adiabatic evolution}

The ground-state energies obtained using VQE and adiabatic quantum evolution (AQC) are compared with the exact ground-state energy $E_0$, calculated by numerical diagonalization. 
The energy error is defined as
\begin{align}
    \Delta E = |E-E_0|.
\end{align}

For the two-dimensional four-qubit Ising model with
$J=1$, $h=0$, and $g=0.01$, the exact ground-state
energy is approximately $E_0=-4.0001$.

In this regime, where $g\ll J$ and $h=0$, the ground state is approximately the GHZ state
\begin{align}
    \ket{\mathrm{GHZ}_n}
    =\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
    \left(\ket{0}^{\otimes n}
    +\ket{1}^{\otimes n}\right).
\end{align}
The fidelity of the final AQC state is therefore also calculated as
\begin{align}
    F_{\mathrm{GHZ}}
    =\bra{\mathrm{GHZ}_n}
    \rho_f
    \ket{\mathrm{GHZ}_n},
\end{align}
where $\rho_f$ is the final density matrix.

Table~\ref{tab:comparison} compares the energy errors with and without simulated quantum noise, for different evolution times $T$ and numbers of Trotter steps $M$.

    \label{tab:comparison}
    \begin{tabular}{lcccc}

In the ideal case, VQE gives a small energy error, but a relatively low GHZ fidelity. 
AQC, on the other hand, achieves a fidelity close to unity, even when its energy error is larger than that of VQE.
This illustrates that a small energy error does not necessarily imply a high fidelity, particularly when the lowest energy levels are close together.

With noise, VQE maintains a relatively small energy error, while AQC becomes less accurate as the number of Trotter steps increases.

\subsection{VQE noise analysis}

To investigate the sensitivity of VQE to quantum noise, phase-flip errors are introduced on RY gates and depolarizing errors on CX gates (cf. Fig.~\ref{fig:state_preparation}).

\begin{figure}[ht]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]
    {Figs/Ry_phase_vs_CX_depolarizing_D2_N4_J1_h0_g1.pdf}
    \caption{Absolute VQE ground-state energy error
    as a function of RY phase-flip and CX depolarizing
    probabilities.}
    \label{fig:vqe_noise}
\end{figure}

For the four-qubit Ising model with $J=1$, $h=0$, and $g=1$, the corresponding error probabilities $p_{\mathrm{RY}}$ and $p_{\mathrm{CX}}$ are varied independently between $0$ and $0.02$.

At each point, VQE is performed and the resulting ground-state energy is compared with the exact value.
The results are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:vqe_noise}.


From Fig.~\ref{fig:vqe_noise}, the energy error increases mainly with the CX depolarizing probability, while the RY phase-flip probability has a smaller effect within the considered range.

The observed energy error contains contributions from both quantum noise and imperfect variational optimization. 
Consequently, a nonzero error is also present in the absence of noise.

\subsection{Adiabatic quantum evolution}
The performance of adiabatic quantum evolution is investigated by varying the total evolution time $T$ and the number of Trotter steps $M$. 
The final-state GHZ fidelity is calculated for ideal evolution and for evolution with a noise model derived from the FakeSherbrooke backend. 
Fig.~\ref{fig:aqc_fidelity} and Fig.~\ref{fig:aqc_fidelity_zoomed} both display the final-state GHZ fidelity  with and without simulated backend noise.

In Fig.~\ref{fig:aqc_fidelity}, $M,T\in[1,1000]$, using a $20\times20$ grid. 
Without noise, the fidelity approaches unity as both $M$ and $T$ increase. 
With noise, on the other hand, the fidelity decreases significantly for larger $M$, particularly for $M\gtrsim200$.

Fig.~\ref{fig:aqc_fidelity_zoomed} shows the GHZ fidelity for $M,T\in[1,100]$, using a $100\times100$ grid. Without noise, the fidelity approaches unity over much of the parameter space, although regions of lower fidelity appear for small numbers of Trotter steps.
This agrees with Fig.~\ref{fig:aqc_fidelity}.

With noise, however, high fidelity is restricted to smaller region, approximately $T\in[5,32]$ and $M\in[5,40]$.
The highest observed fidelity, $F_{\mathrm{GHZ}}=0.951$, is achieved with $T=10$ and $M=15$.

Increasing $M$ at fixed $T$ improves the Trotter approximation but also increases the number of noisy quantum gates. 
Conversely, increasing $T$ at fixed $M$ increases the time step $\Delta t=T/M$, potentially introducing larger Trotter errors.

The results therefore illustrate the trade-off between adiabatic evolution, Trotter approximation errors, and accumulated quantum noise.



\section{Discussion and conclusion}

VQE and digitized adiabatic evolution have been compared using the transverse-field Ising model.

In the ideal simulations, both methods give good estimates of the ground-state energy, although AQC achieves a significantly higher GHZ fidelity for the parameters considered. 
With simulated noise, however, VQE gives smaller energy errors, while AQC is strongly affected by increasing circuit depth.

The results demonstrate the importance of considering quantum noise when comparing quantum algorithms. 
The simulations are limited to small systems, and further work could include larger systems and methods for reducing the effects of noise.
